Playing in your voice

The caller speaks

Any phone, any app, any language. NoteFIsh hears which language on the first phrase and captions it in yours — with how they sound, not only what they said.

You just talk

Hold the key and answer the way you’d answer a colleague. No typing, no script, no phrasebook. Let go, and it’s on its way.

It hears how you said it

Text decides nothing about tone. The desk measures your clip — loud, fast, gentle — against your own baseline, weighs the caller’s mood, and picks one of your six takes.

They hear you

Your own voice, in their language, with the feeling you had. Half-duplex, so nobody talks over anybody. If a reply can’t be confirmed as played, the desk tells you.

Built for the floor

Built for the shift you work, not the demo.

80+ languages, found on the first phrase

Leave “Partner speaks” on Auto. The caller talks; the desk hears which language it is and answers in it. Captions still arrive in yours.

Six takes of your voice

One short passage per feeling — calm, warm, energetic, reassuring, apologetic, firm. On the call, NoteFIsh picks the take that matches how you spoke. Or you do.

Canned lines, one tap

The things you say on every call. One tap speaks them to the caller, translated, in your voice — while your hands stay on the ticket.

Make it sound like your team

A line of house style shapes how replies are worded — length, politeness, phrasing. Facts, numbers and promises are locked.

Your desk, your layout

Drag panels between two columns, hide what you don’t use, keep three presets. The layout is saved to your seat, so it follows you to any machine on the floor.

A floor, not a hero desk

Seats, a queue, ring-all with first-answer-wins. Every agent has their own voice takes, canned lines and layout; supervisors see who is on what without hearing a word of audio.

Your voice stays yours.

A cloned voice is a serious thing. Cloning needs your explicit consent; the caller’s voice is never cloned; credentials never reach a browser, and the transcript stays with the call in your own system of record.

Does the caller hear a robot?

No. They hear a clone of your voice, made from a minute of your own reading, speaking their language. The feeling — calm, warm, apologetic — comes from how you actually said it, measured on your clip, and from one of six takes you recorded.

You have a way with words. Now, in every language.
